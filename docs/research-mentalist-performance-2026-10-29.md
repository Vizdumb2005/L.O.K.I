## What the performances actually do

### The "questions" are a conversation, not a filter
[TRANSCRIPT, source 2] Derren's narrowing: "color-wise you've got red or black
so which would you rather have" ... "not three cuz there are three of us" ...
then "happy that was a free choice? okay great". Constraints arrive with
playful, personal reasons; the spectator is asked to confirm comfort, not to
feed a form. [TRANSCRIPT, source 6 performance section] the same binary
narrowing ("remove red or black", "absorb hearts or diamonds", "toss pictures
or numbers") run as a game with the spectator filling in blanks. [VISUAL,
source 6 t=2:31] the narrowing is delivered seated across from a spectator in
a casual demo setup, with the caption "card no free yeah fair" on screen.

[HYPOTHESIS] This is the single biggest gap vs. LOKI: our probes are rendered
as a questionnaire whose answers visibly compute the reveal. Same information,
completely different felt experience.

### Method is hidden; the visible steps are ritual
[TRANSCRIPT, source 1] "spread the cards... remember one... burn the image of
that card in your mind", "in your mind not out loud repeat that card to
yourself over and over". [VISUAL, source 1 t=1:43] the "repeat that card"
instruction appears in the on-screen caption during a live close-up.
[TRANSCRIPT, source 2] cards checked in new-deck order, shuffled by the
participant under the table, "at no point... am I going to touch those cards".
[TRANSCRIPT, source 3] "I took a pack of cards out... put them down and I'm
never going to touch them again". [VISUAL, source 3 t=2:22] real footage with
a live participant reaction ("HE'S A WITCH" caption). Fairness theater makes
the method's hiding place feel empty.

### Story carries the procedure (time misdirection)
[TRANSCRIPT, source 2] while the participant shuffles and reverses a card
under the table, Derren tells three long "tales of coincidence", themed to the
couple's relationship. The card handling is incidental; the story is the show.
[TAUGHT, source 7] misdirection is "the story the audience tells themselves"
(Penn & Teller's definition), NOT pure distraction: a spectator who notices
they missed something feels the story break. It works best while the spectator
is focused. [VISUAL, source 7 t=3:20] the chapter on screen at that point is
"The psychology of attention".

### The spectator performs the reveal
[TRANSCRIPT, source 3] the participant deals and counts aloud to the 11th card
and turns it over. [TRANSCRIPT, source 2] participants spread the deck and
turn the reversed card themselves. In these two transcripts the reveal is put
in the spectator's hands rather than announced by the performer.

### Wobble, then kicker
[TRANSCRIPT, source 1] staged apparent failure ("there is no king of
diamonds"), then the impossible souvenir kicker. [VISUAL, source 1 t=2:28] the
kicker lands as a visible spectator reaction ("oh for God sake" caption).
[TRANSCRIPT, source 4] Max Maven openly teases method knowledge ("the
psychological force was three... if I'd gone for one he would have been
toast... do you want to stay with two?") - daring the spectator to change
their mind, which deepens the free-choice feeling, then the kicker lands in
his own pocket. [VISUAL, source 4 t=4:16] the spectator holds the card at the
kicker moment.

## What the teachers say the methods are

- [TAUGHT, source 5] Classic force: glimpse the target, hold a break, time the
  spread so the target arrives under the spectator's finger; "aim the deck at
  the place where it's easiest for them to touch your card". A force is
  choreography + timing; the spectator feels a free touch. [VISUAL, source 5
  t=1:00 and t=2:20] hands spread the deck while the captions give the timing
  instruction ("spread the cards and try to time it when they want") and "you
  can touch any card" - the choreography is shown, not just described.
- [TAUGHT, source 6] Equivoque (magician's choice): every possible answer has
  a prepared interpretation, so any choice advances the same outcome ("the
  trick is that we have a backup sentence for anything that they say"). Rules:
  identical verbiage between question and handling or "the inconsistency
  begin[s] to peek out"; leave blanks for the spectator; confidence and
  pauses. [VISUAL, source 6 t=2:31 and t=5:03] demonstrated with a spectator
  first, then taught to camera.
- [TAUGHT, source 6] Recommendation for deeper study: Peter Turner,
  "Psychological Card Forces".
- [TAUGHT, source 7] Eye-line/gaze of the performer steers audience attention;
  the audience's own wrong prediction is what creates the impact. [VISUAL,
  source 7 t=1:02] taught to camera with a coin-transfer example ("all my
  attention went here").

## Integrity rules for our adaptation (from main-agent guardrails)

1. A "sealed prediction" must be genuinely fixed before any choices (e.g. a
   committed hash revealed afterwards). No retroactive sealing - that would be
   false fairness theater, not open illusion.
2. Equivoque-style absorption must not covertly contradict the user's real
   choices, and must not be the same questionnaire wearing decorative copy. If
   answers claim to guide the reading, they must actually guide it.
3. The consent screen already frames LOKI as an illusion; nothing in the
   adaptation may claim real mind reading, emotion detection, or lie detection.

## Feasible digital adaptations

[ADAPTATION - feasible now, no new sensing]
- Reframe probes as one-at-a-time conversational offers with reasons and
  performer commentary; absorb any answer truthfully (the engine really uses
  it) while the patter - not the UI - does the narrowing work.
- Narrative beats during build phases (performer tells a short themed story
  instead of showing a progress feel).
- User-performed reveal: the user spreads/turns/counts to the answer
  themselves; LOKI never just announces it.
- Psychological priors (existing research brief: measured card-choice biases)
  and choice-architecture salience as the digital analogue of forces -
  disclosed in docs, never claimed as mind reading.
- Honest wobble: a staged struggle is only legitimate when the method actually
  knows; real misses keep the existing honest-miss path.
- Sealed prediction via commit-then-reveal hash when a prediction is claimed.

[ADAPTATION - unresolved / not validated]
- Webcam gaze as card evidence: zone-level only; earlier ~2-4 deg error figures
  come from literature, not from validation on this device or users. The
  current lab is uncalibrated and explicitly gated off. Any future gaze input
  needs a calibration flow and measured accuracy before feeding the show, and
  must never be presented as card reading until validated.
- "Separate the subject's voice from background voices": client-side noise
  suppression and voice-activity detection are feasible; true speaker
  separation in a browser is not a solved capability. Do not label a noise
  filter as speaker separation.
- Mandatory camera/mic entry: implementable as an explicit permission gate
  with honest unsupported-device handling; sensors add presence/attention and
  a spoken-answer channel, not card inference, until validation proves more.
